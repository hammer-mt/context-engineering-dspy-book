Why this book

Stop tinkering with prompt strings

As models have become better at reasoning, the wording of a prompt matters less than the inputs the model has to reason through. Context engineering is the discipline of curating what a language model receives so it performs well without wasting tokens or making errors.

DSPy is the framework for putting that into practice. You declare what your system should do as inputs and outputs, pair it with a dataset and an evaluation metric, and DSPy’s prompt optimizers improve the program for you. The result is portable across models, built from maintainable modules, and improved through evaluation instead of guesswork.

  • 24% → 51%success on question answering with search
  • 66% → 87%accuracy on classification tasks
  • +10%document retrieval quality
  • 10% to 100%+improvements in the author’s own consulting work

Chapter 3

DSPy in eight steps

The process the book uses for every DSPy program. The chapter applies it to a task with no obvious metric, rewriting AI-generated text to sound human, by training an LLM judge first and then optimizing against it. GEPA takes the judge from 85% to 100% accuracy and the rewriter from 0% to 40%.

  1. Specify your SignaturesDeclare the task as inputs and outputs instead of a prompt.
  2. Build your modulesWrap each Signature in an inference strategy such as ChainOfThought.
  3. Explore a few examplesRun the program, inspect the history and read the traces.
  4. Collect your datasetLoad examples, convert them to DSPy format, split train and test.
  5. Define your metricsStart with exact match, then add an LLM judge for fuzzy tasks.
  6. Establish a baselineEvaluate the judge and the task before changing anything.
  7. Optimize your programLet GEPA rewrite the instructions against your metric.
  8. Test and iterateTry BestOfN, swap in another model, and measure again.
